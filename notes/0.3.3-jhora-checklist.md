# 0.3.3 — JHora spot-check of transits and Sade Sati (checklist for Adithya)

Prepared by Claude Code in step 0.3.2. About 30–45 minutes in JHora on the Windows laptop.

## What you are checking

Jathag now works out **when** Saturn, Jupiter and Rahu/Ketu change sign. To check a date you don't need
any special JHora feature. You make a chart for a moment **30 minutes before** the change and another
for **30 minutes after** it, and look at which sign the planet is in each time. If JHora shows the
"before" sign at the first time and the "after" sign at the second time, Jathag's moment is right to
within half an hour. That is far more precise than any date we will ever show.

## Set-up (once)

1. Open JHora with the same settings you used for the reference births (Lahiri ayanamsa, **mean node**
   for Rahu/Ketu; the file `tests/reference/raw/jhora-settings-jathag.ini`). The mean node matters for
   check 5.
2. For every check, enter these as the "birth" details (they are only a moment in time, not a person):
   - **Place:** New Delhi, latitude **28 N 37**, longitude **77 E 13**
   - **Time zone:** **+5:30** (IST, east of Greenwich)
   - **Date and time:** as given in the table below (24-hour clock, IST)
3. Look at the planet's **sign** in the Rasi (D-1) chart or in the planet list. Ignore degrees. Only
   the sign matters.

The place does not change the result: a planet changes sign at the same moment everywhere. Only the
clock reading differs. New Delhi on IST is used so that every time below is a normal Indian clock time.

### If JHora has its own transit or Sade Sati view

JHora may have a transit view (often called "Transits" or "Gochara"; it can sit in a menu or as an
option on the chart). It may also list Sade Sati periods, and it may have a search for the next time a
planet enters a sign. If you find one within about 10 minutes, write down what it says for R01's
current Sade Sati (start, peak, setting, end) and for checks 1–5. **Don't rely on it**: such a view can
use a different rule (for example, starting a phase on the last entry instead of the first, or
splitting a period at each dip). The before/after method above is the real test. Any difference in a
JHora table is useful information for the Project, not automatically a mistake.
